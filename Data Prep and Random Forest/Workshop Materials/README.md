Hello cosmicAI students!

Make sure to pip install `requirements.txt` first! If you load all the required packages into a python environment, you should be able to run all the projects in this folder.

There are four folders in this repository. They all have preliminary datasets and notebooks taken from astronomers around the world studying different astrophysical processes! The data, notebooks and projects in this repository are for the **data prep, random forests and unsupervised learning** weeks.

1. In `classifying_alerts` we look at how to find strongly lensed active galactic nuclei (AGN) from the Vera Rubin Observatory LSST alert stream.
2. In `galaxy_agn_classification` we attempt to understand ionization processes in galaxies vs active galactic nuclei (AGN) that live at the heart of galaxies. They both have different ionization mechanisms and it is important to classify one from the other. Traditionally, people have used empirical cutoffs to classify data, and here we explore random forests vs unsupervised learning and see how well both methods fare on *different wavelength data*.
3. In `light_curve_classification`, we work with **time-series data**. Here, we look at how astronomical sources change in brightness over time, and classify one type of objects from another (for example, exploding stars -- supernovae -- look very different from periodically varying stars!). This was initially a Kaggle challenge that we have adopted for use in this workshop.
4. In `tde_host_galaxy` we try to find galaxies that are most likely to host black hole that tidally distrupt stars! There are some galaxies where we see *tidal distruption events (TDE)* more often. A TDE is when a star is broken down by a black hole at the center of a galaxy -- the stellar debris can form a disk around the star, affect star formation in the galaxy, or it can preferentially happen in galaxies that have had a lot of star formation happen recently -- anything could be true!
